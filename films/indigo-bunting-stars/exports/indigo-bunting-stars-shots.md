# North Is Where the Sky Stands Still — shots

1. 0–2 s, first summer: a young indigo bunting, drawn as a constellation, perches under the northern sky and looks up at Polaris while the Dippers draw on.
2. 2–4 s, the sky turns: the whole sky wheels counter-clockwise around Polaris, every star drawing its trail; the pole alone stays still.
3. 4–5.5 s, the star that stays: the camera closes in on Polaris; the pointer stars of the Big Dipper lead to it, five gaps beyond Dubhe.
4. 5.5–7 s, autumn funnel (negative): Emlen's funnel from above, white paper and black ink; the restless bird's footprints cluster on the side away from the pole — south.
5. 7–8.5 s, the false pole: in a planetarium the sky turns around Betelgeuse instead, and the young bird learns that.
6. 8.5–10 s, the funnel again (negative): now the footprints point away from Betelgeuse.
7. 10–12 s, south: under the true sky the bunting flies away from Polaris, and the film loops.

Source: Stephen Emlen's indigo bunting experiments (Auk 1967; Science 1970).
